About the data

Methodology and credits

The SCS Developments Tracker continues Andrew Chubb's Maritime Assertiveness Time Series (MATS). MATS 1.0 coded assertive moves by China, Vietnam and the Philippines in the South China Sea from 1970 to 2015. This project keeps that coding scheme unchanged, carries the series forward to the present, extends it to Malaysia, Indonesia and Taiwan, and adds an event layer beneath each move with dates, locations and sources.

What counts as a move

A move is an intensification of a claimant's assertiveness compared with the previous calendar year, shown by one of three criteria: qualitative novelty, increased frequency, or expanded geographic scope. An action that merely continues the previous year's behaviour at the same level is not a new move; it is covered by the earlier move's estimated duration. Only non-cooperative behaviour is coded; conciliatory actions are deliberately excluded.

Where the data come from

The 1970–2015 moves for China, Vietnam and the Philippines are Chubb's MATS 1.02, loaded unchanged. The full methodology, including source lists, verification practice and inter-coder checks, is in the appendix to Chubb (2021), released under CC0 and kept as the project codebook in the repository.

Each claimant's 1970 level is read against a baseline of its known actions before 1970: Chubb's own list for China, and lists compiled by this project for the other five (BASELINE-*.md on the Downloads page), each row with its source.

For later years and for the additional claimants, scheduled runs harvest a documented list of sources and propose candidate events; a human coder decides what enters the dataset. Candidates compiled by hand from sources enter the same queue and are labelled by their compiler rather than by a model. Every machine suggestion is kept beside the coder's final decision so that agreement can be measured (the report is below). Project-coded moves are labelled as such wherever they appear.

Every change to the dataset is a reviewed, versioned commit in the public repository, and an automatic check refuses any change that would alter the 1970–2015 figures for the original three claimants.

How good are the machine's suggestions

Every candidate event the harvester proposes carries a suggested coding (Type, Domains, Issues, dyads and, should an extractor propose one, the move it belongs to) and the name of the extractor that made it. The coder's decision is final; the suggestion stays on the row. This report compares the two for every accepted event with a suggestion, by extractor version and by the year of the event, and is regenerated on every build (agreement.json). It is a measure of the suggestions, not of the dataset: the dataset holds only what a coder decided.

How to cite

Any use of these data must cite one or more of:

  • Chubb, A. (2022), Dynamics of Assertiveness in the South China Sea: China, the Philippines, and Vietnam, 1970–2015 (Washington, D.C.: National Bureau of Asian Research).
  • Chubb, A. (2021), “PRC Assertiveness in the South China Sea: Measuring Continuity and Change, 1970–2015,” International Security 45(3): 79–121. doi:10.1162/isec_a_00400
  • Chubb, A. (2022), “Maritime Assertiveness Time Series (MATS 1.0)” dataset, version 1.01, May 18 (the author's suggested citation; the file supplied to this project is version 1.02).
