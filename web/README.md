# Public site

A self-contained static bundle built from `data/*.csv` by `python -m web.build`. No framework, no build chain: Jinja2 templates, one stylesheet, three scripts, two vendored fonts (Outfit and Instrument Sans, both OFL). Relative paths only, so the output folder can be dropped onto any host (GitHub Pages for now; ASPI later) or opened from disk.

## Pages

- `index.html` home: lede, headline numbers, the event map (compact filters), the latest developments, the overview charts; `issues-domains.html` (issue and domain mentions by year, by claimant and by sub-region); `subregions.html` (choropleth by period with a claimant selector, moves by sub-region and type, one chart per sub-region over time); `cases.html` searchable case browser (client-side filtering of an inline index); `cases/<move_id>.html` one page per move with coding, events, sources and an inset map of the sub-regions (simplified polygons inlined, ~1 KB); `map.html` event map (MapLibre GL, vendored; events, polygons and the basemap style inline, the events also written to `data/events.json`; OpenFreeMap's vector tiles, fonts and sprites are the only external requests — free, no key, no stated limits; the bundled style `web/static/vendor/openfreemap/positron-en.json` labels everything in English/Latin script, and the tile URL inside it is the one line to change for another tile host); `methodology.html` (codebook summary, provenance, how to cite, and the machine–human agreement report, also written to `data/agreement.json`); `downloads.html` (the files at the latest release tag, with checksums, served from `downloads/` inside the bundle, plus citations in text, BibTeX and CFF).

## Design notes

Subject: a research dataset about contested waters. Audience: researchers, analysts, journalists. Job: trends at a glance, then down to the evidence.

- **Tokens (tickets 15, 16):** the block at the top of `style.css` holds the brand: display face and its weights, body face, the kicker style, paper, surface, the sea colours (`--sea-deep`, `--sea`, `--sea-mid`, `--sea-light`, `--lagoon`, `--foam`), band, panel, ink, accent, focus, the map's polygon colours, radius, measure and spacing. Its values were read from three references the maintainer supplied (2026-10-26): a programme banner (a deep navy-to-teal sea from above, a light geometric sans title in white with a short rule under it, dashed boundary lines), the hosting institute's article pages (white paper, bold sans headlines under a small letter-spaced kicker, grey standfirsts, hairline rules) and the original dashboard (near-black panels with white labels around a light-grey map canvas). The visual language is adopted; no institution's name, logo, lion mark, wordmark or photograph is in the site's pages, styles or assets (the project documents served under `downloads/` name the intended host, as they must), and a seam-3 test checks the built bundle for them. `map.js` reads the polygon colours from the tokens. The data palettes below the block do not follow the brand.
- **Identity device:** the sea. One dark plane carries the masthead on every page and, on the home page, the hero: a navy-to-teal gradient with an inline SVG drawing (the foam of a swell at the top left, lagoon blooms where a reef rises at the right, faint contours, dashed boundary lines) — drawn, not photographed, and coloured from the tokens. The brand name is small bold letter-spaced capitals beside the reticle mark (circle, cross-hair, centre dot; an original drawing). The event map sits directly under the hero inside a full-bleed near-black panel in the dashboard's manner (white labels and chips, the light-grey canvas inside); the rest of the page is white paper, hairlines, data and type. Lists are rows divided by hairlines, not cards; figures keep a thin frame because they are the one thing on the page that is a picture.
- **Home page:** kicker, title (light display weight, the short rule under it), lede and three headline numbers side by side on the sea, then the event map in its panel, whose canvas is sized to the viewport (`100vh − 300px`, at least 420px) so that at least half of a 900px-tall desktop window is map and at least 40 % of a 720px one (a window under 780px tall drops the numbers' footnotes); one compact filter row (claimant chips, type; the full set lives on the map page), then the latest developments (the newest accepted event per move; MATS-era seed dates outside their move's year are left out; project coding marked; the caption says when the list is still MATS-era only), then the charts and the four types.
- **Type:** Outfit (a light geometric sans, close to the banner's title face) for titles, h2, the headline numbers, dates in the latest list and move descriptions — weight 300 on the sea, 600 as article headlines on paper; Instrument Sans for interface, labels, charts and body. The one all-caps style is the kicker (small, bold, letter-spaced, in the manner of the wordmark): above page and section titles, on the numbers' labels, the brand name and the footer headings.
- **Colour:** page plane white (the article pages), ink blue-black, links and kickers in the sea's teal (6:1 on white), focus and hover in the dashboard's amber; the masthead and hero the sea's navy, the map panel near-black. Claimants use the dataviz categorical palette in fixed slot order (China, Vietnam, Philippines, Malaysia, Indonesia, Taiwan) so a filter never repaints survivors. Move types are an ordered intensity scale and use one blue ramp, pale (declarative) to dark (use of force). Issues and domains use the categorical slots in fixed order on their own page. The choropleth uses the sequential blue ramp, reversed in dark mode so zero recedes toward the surface. All palettes validated with the dataviz validator in light and dark mode.
- **Marks:** stacked columns ≤ 24px wide, 4px rounded data-end, surface-coloured gaps between segments (2px, 1px when bands are narrow), hairline solid gridlines, muted axis ticks, tabular figures on ticks and in tables.
- **Pills in the case list:** a dashed pill marks a move pending the author's confirmation; a solid pill marks project coding (any move not from MATS 1.02), so the two provenances never pass for one another.
- **Case browser filters:** within a group (type, domain, issue) any checked value matches; across groups all must match; the claimant chips are a show/hide set like the overview's, so unchecking all shows nothing.
- **Interaction:** one hover tooltip per column (values lead, labels follow, line keys), keyboard focus shows the same; each figure's filters sit directly above it (on the home page the map's claimant chips and the charts' are independent sets); every chart has a table-view twin (the WCAG-clean equivalent, and the relief for the light-mode hues under 3:1 contrast: Philippines, Malaysia, Indonesia and the declarative step). Keyboard users read the table; the column hit targets are pointer-only so a chart is not 46 tab stops.
- **Event map:** events are the move's colour (claimant); a filled disc has its own coordinates, a hollow one stands at a named feature; events sharing a position stack into one disc carrying their count; unplaced events are a neutral count pill on their sub-region. The basemap is OpenFreeMap's Positron with English-only labels (a Chinese, Thai or Vietnamese label would be a second script competing with the data), desaturated in light mode and inverted-and-retoned in dark mode so the data stays the loudest thing on the map. Time is one month slider plus a window selector rather than a two-handle range: one control, and "the year ending here" is how the dataset thinks.
- **Responsive:** columns shrink to a 5px band before the chart scrolls (46 years fit a phone); if it must scroll it opens at the latest years with a fade on the left edge. Small multiples go to one column below 440px.

Tried and rejected: three-column small multiples on desktop (bands too thin to read); hover targets only on years with data (a year with none should still answer); an 8px minimum band on phones (hid 2007–2015 behind an unsignposted scroll); focusable column hit targets (184 tab stops per page).
