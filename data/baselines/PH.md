# Philippines: baseline actions before 1970

Scope note. This list is the Philippine counterpart to Chubb's PRC "zero-baseline" list: known non-cooperative acts by which the Philippine state asserted (or exercised jurisdiction in support of) a claim in the South China Sea between independence (4 July 1946) and the start of the MATS series in 1970. Each row carries a source actually opened for this exercise and a verbatim quote of 25 words or fewer; dates are given as the cited source gives them. Private acts (Tomás Cloma's 1956 "Freedomland") appear only through the government's response to them, and the response is characterised (backing vs. toleration vs. disavowal). Two systematic caveats: (1) the Philippine record before 1970 is thin and several secondary accounts contradict each other on who said what and when, so the "Source" column names the account that was checked rather than asserting an independent fact; (2) the 1968-71 occupation dates are contested, and all variants found are set out in the dedicated note below the table.

Sub-region key: Spratlys (incl. Kalayaan/"New Southern Islands"), Scarborough (Bajo de Masinloc), Other (archipelago-wide / treaty-limits acts bearing on SCS waters).

## Note on the 1968-1971 occupation dates (conflicting sources)

Recorded as each source states it; none of the sources opened gives a primary document for the first landing.

- **1968 — "troops sent to the area"**: UP Primer (Baviera & Batongbacal 2013, p. 21): "The Philippines started sending troops to the area in 1968." Heydarian (Diplomat 2024): same. Wikipedia "Nanshan Island" (pointer only, cites an ABC 2014 feature): "inhabited since 1968 when the Philippines occupied this island." Wikipedia "Philippines and the Spratly Islands" (pointer only, uncited): "The Philippines sent troops to the Spratly group for the first time in 1968."
- **"Late 1960s"**: McDevitt foreword to Rosen (CNA 2014), p. iv: West York, Nanshan, Flat and Lankiam Cay occupied "in the late 1960s" (the same foreword's "upwards of 60 years" for Thitu/Loaita/Northeast Cay is internally inconsistent).
- **1970 — Nanshan (Lawak) and Flat (Patag)**: UP Primer pp. 23-24: "The Philippines posted troops on Lawak island in 1970, followed with Kota, Likas, Pag-asa and Parola islands in 1971." NMDIS/China Daily timeline 1970-79 (https://subsites.chinadaily.com.cn/SouthChinaSeaen/2017-03/15/c_71501.htm), 1970 entry: "The Philippine Navy invaded and occupied China's Feixin Dao and Mahuan Dao, illegally named them and garrisoned troops thereon." Quezon III (quezon.ph, 11 Mar 2026): "the Philippines posted troops on Lawak Island" (sequenced c. 1970).
- **1970-71 — three islands**: Dzurek 1996, p. 20 (citing Hamzah 1990; Heinzig 1976): Philippine forces occupied "three Spratly islands in the Kalayaan area" during 1970-71 as Palawan oil exploration began. Hung (NPF Taiwan 2009, part II): "The Philippines occupied three Spratly islets" in 1970-71.
- **1971 — Thitu (Pag-asa), Loaita (Kota), West York (Likas), Northeast Cay (Parola)**: NMDIS timeline 1970-79: 14-18 Apr 1971 "the Philippine Navy invaded and occupied Nanyao Dao and Zhongye Dao of China successively"; 11 Jul 1971 "the Philippines had occupied three islands (namely, Zhongye Dao, Mahuan Dao and Feixin Dao)"; 30 Jul 1971 "the Philippine Army invaded and occupied Xiyue Dao and Beizi Dao." Sellars 2017, p. 240 (citing 1973MANILA00858): at Marcos's July 1971 press conference the Philippines was in "effective occupation and control of Nanshan Island (in Tagalog, Lawak), Thitu (Pagasa), and Flat Island (Patag)"; expansion to five features (adding West York, Northeast Cay, Loaita) over the following 2.5 years.
- **1974 — Thitu**: AMTI/CSIS Island Tracker (https://amti.csis.org/thitu-island/): "The Philippines first took possession of the feature in 1974." (Outlier; contradicts Sellars/US Embassy 1973 cable and the Chinese timeline.)

Reading: the best-attested sequence is Nanshan and Flat Island in 1970 (first Philippine garrisons, i.e. the first MATS-era "move"), Thitu and Loaita in April 1971, West York and Northeast Cay in July 1971, publicly acknowledged by Marcos on 10 July 1971. The "1968" date appears only in Philippine-side secondary sources and Wikipedia; treat it as an unverified lead for a possible reconnaissance/first deployment, not as the first occupation.

## Unverified leads

- **1955–56 Foreign Office deliberation on occupying Spratly features (US INR, *Islands of the South China Sea*, Aug 1956).** The CIA Reading Room record page (https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/node/2006279) and the document PDF (CIA-RDP08C01297R000300180019-2) were not retrievable on 2026-10-18 (both redirect to the site's home page), so the quoted passage "has considered occupying and laying claim to one or more of the islands" could not be checked and the item is kept here rather than in the table.

- **1946 Quirino "New Southern Islands" declaration**: Wikipedia ("Philippines and the Spratly Islands") says Vice-President Quirino "reiterated the 'New Southern Islands'" claim in 1946, citing a Diplomat article (https://thediplomat.com/2010/11/aquinos-spratly-islands-call/) that is undated, unattributed and misdates Cloma to 1957. Only the MacArthur letter (table row 1) is sourced to scholarship (Valero 1993 via the UP Primer). The attybulao.com compilation's claim that the Philippine position was "first expressed in the United Nations General Assembly in 1946" is uncited.
- **1949 (Quirino) "would not press claim while Taiwan held the Spratlys"**: Wikipedia ("Free Territory of Freedomland") attributes to Quirino, 1950; no checked scholarly source states it.
- **1956 Philippine notes/replies to the ROC, PRC and RVN protests**: Dzurek, Bernas and Rosen confirm the protests (PRC statement 29 May 1956; ROC and RVN protests); no source opened gives the text or date of a Philippine *reply*. The only documented government actions toward Taipei in 1956 are conciliatory: NMDIS timeline, 7 Jul 1956: "upon the notice of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Philippines, Thomas Cloma led 3 students..." to return the ROC flag taken from Itu Aba; Hung (NPF): Dec 1956 disavowal of Cloma's voyage. ROC note of 3 Jun 1957 protesting Cloma's 13 May 1957 re-landing on Northeast Cay (NMDIS) — Philippine response not found.
- **Alleged 1957 Garcia "recognition" of the Free Territory of Freedomland**: Bernas (2011) reports a former Philippine diplomat's claim that Garcia committed in 1957 "that the Philippines recognized the Free Territory of Freedomland"; Bernas treats it as unproven. Not corroborated.
- **Garcia 1956 reply "occupation and proximity ... no reasons these islands should not be under Philippine jurisdiction"**: Wikipedia paraphrase of a letter to Cloma; consistent with the INR report's "saw no reason why the islands should not belong to the Philippines" but the exact letter and date were not located.
- **1958/1960 UNCLOS declarations (Tolentino)**: Quezon III states Tolentino "appealed for special treatment" for the treaty-limits claim at UNCLOS II on 15 Mar 1960 and that the Philippines abstained on the US-Canada 6+6 proposal; no primary record opened. Treaty-limits, not SCS-specific.
- **1968 Philippine naval reconnaissance of the Spratlys**: searched specifically; no source found describing a 1968 reconnaissance as such. The only 1968 items are the undocumented "sent troops" claim (table) and the UN ECAFE seabed survey that the Philippine literature uses to motivate the 1970-71 occupations.
- **Marcos statements on Kalayaan before 1970**: none found. Earliest Marcos statement located is the 10 July 1971 Malacañang communiqué (Arreglado 1982, App. S, via UP Primer n.59; NMDIS 10 Jul 1971: "It was for the first time that the Philippines brazenly claimed sovereignty over Nansha Qundao on a public occasion"). Romulo's advice to develop the features for defence (Quezon III) is undated.
- **1957 Scarborough oceanographic survey**: single source (Rosen 2014); Batongbacal and Bonnet, who worked from Philippine archives, give the 1961 PCGS survey instead. Possibly a conflation.
- **Scarborough 1965 flag/lighthouse — Bonnet's dating**: Bonnet (2012, p. 21 n.63) places the flag and lighthouse after the 1964 smuggling investigations but gives no year; the 1965 date rests on the DFA position paper (2012) and sources that follow it.
- **"Navy arrested foreign fishermen" at Scarborough pre-1970**: Bonnet says the Navy arrested Filipino fishermen involved in the 1963 smuggling; no pre-1970 arrest of foreign nationals found.
