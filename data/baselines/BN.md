# Brunei: baseline actions before 1970

**Scope note.** The claimant is the Sultanate of Brunei (Negara Brunei Darussalam), a British protected state under the 1888 agreement until full independence on 1 January 1984. Under the 1959 agreement the Sultanate ran its own internal government, including oil licensing, while the United Kingdom conducted its foreign affairs and defence. Following decision 03 (amended 2026-11-23), acts of the Sultanate's own government are listed as Brunei's, and acts of the British Crown for Brunei are labelled "colonial" and kept as context. The pre-1970 record is jurisdictional and petroleum-licensing in character: the Sultan's 1954 continental-shelf proclamation, the 1958 British Orders in Council that fixed the sea boundaries with Sarawak and North Borneo out to the 100-fathom line, and the first offshore exploration and licensing. No pre-1970 Bruneian statement, patrol or marker concerning Louisa Reef or other Spratly features was found, apart from a reported 1948 wildlife-sanctuary designation of Louisa Reef that could not be checked against a primary text. Brunei claims only one Spratly feature, Louisa Reef, as lying on its continental shelf (Roach 2014).

**After 1969 (for orientation; these come to review as candidates, not baseline).** The UK protested to Malaysia on Brunei's behalf over Malaysia's 1979 continental-shelf map in August 1980 and May 1981 (Haller-Trost 1994, p. 38). Brunei enacted 200-mile fishery limits from 1 January 1983 and a 12-mile territorial sea from 10 February 1983, published territorial-waters (1987), continental-shelf and fishery-limits (1988) maps, proclaimed an EEZ in July 1993, awarded deepwater Blocks J and K in 2002–03, settled its boundaries with Malaysia by an Exchange of Letters on 16 March 2009, lodged preliminary information on its outer continental shelf on 12 May 2009, and issued South China Sea statements on 20 July 2020 and 2 September 2023.

## Unverified leads

- The 1948 Louisa Reef sanctuary is cited by Roach (2014) from ReefBase (http://mpa.reefbase.org/database.aspx); no Bruneian gazette text was found.
- A Brunei Shell offshore concession of the early 1960s is said to have extended to the 100-fathom line; the agreement text was not found.
