# Malaysia: baseline actions before 1970

**Scope note.** The claimant is the Federation of Malaya (independent 31 August 1957), succeeded by Malaysia from 16 September 1963, when Sarawak and Sabah (ex-North Borneo) joined; acts of the British Crown for the colonies of Sarawak and North Borneo before 1963 are listed as context and labelled "colonial", since they created the continental-shelf jurisdiction Malaysia inherited. The pre-1970 record is thin and almost entirely jurisdictional-legislative and petroleum-licensing in character: the scholarly consensus (e.g. Lai, Kuik and Abdullah 2021) is that Malaysia's claim to Spratly features was "officially marked" only by the two-sheet continental shelf map of 21 December 1979, and the earliest documented Malaysian naval/marking activity in the Spratlys dates to 1974–75. No verifiable 1960s Malaysian statement, patrol or marker concerning James Shoal, the Luconia Shoals, Swallow Reef, Commodore Reef or Amboyna Cay was found; the entries below should therefore be read as a near-zero feature-specific baseline, with the 1954–1969 shelf and territorial-sea instruments as the only substantive "moves". The 1969 Indonesia–Malaysia shelf agreement is cooperative and is listed as context, not as an assertive act.

**Dating the Spratly claim (for the scope note).** Lai, Kuik and Abdullah 2021 (https://ics.um.edu.my/img/files/Lai-Kuik-Amy.pdf, p. 201) describe the 21 Dec 1979 map as "The most important legislative move, which officially marked Malaysia laying its claims on portions of the Spratly archipelago", and date the first armed-forces directive to 1974 and the first marker expedition to "early 1975". Roach (CNA 2014, https://www.cna.org/reports/2014/malaysia-and-brunei-claims-in-scs): "Malaysia's involvement the Spratlys was the result of its continental shelf claim of 1979". B.A. Hamzah (RSIS, 1 Jul 2014, https://rsis.edu.sg/rsis-publication/rsis/chinas-james-shoal-claim-malaysia-the-undisputed-owner/) treats the 1969 Indonesia treaty as the instrument "which has placed James Shoal on the Malaysian side", but gives no pre-1970 Malaysian act directed at the shoal.

## Unverified leads

- **Full text of the Sarawak (Alteration of Boundaries) Order in Council 1954** at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1954/839 could not be fetched (access blocked in this session); the operative wording above is taken from the UTM slide deck, and the S.I. number/date from LawPlayer. The FAOLEX copy of the North Borneo Order (mal197818.pdf) contains both "24th June, 1954" and a Buckingham Palace date of "24th September, 1954"; the June date matches the companion Sarawak Order and is used here, but the text should be checked against the printed S.I.
- **UK Savingram of 5 June 1954** publicly announcing the 1954 shelf extensions for North Borneo, Sarawak and Brunei (cited in the UTM slides and in Abdullah & Lai 2022, p. 184–185); not opened.
- **Sarawak Shell offshore concession/lease terms (1950s–60s).** Sorkhabi (geoexpro) gives 1955 marine seismic and a 1960 decision to explore offshore Sarawak but no concession instrument or date; Moorthy (fn. 16, quoting Salleh Abas) says only that "a number of licences and leases were issued in Sabah and Sarawak". A dated Sarawak offshore licence pre-1970 was not found.
- **Esso/Conoco offshore concessions off the east coast of Peninsular Malaysia (1968)** under the Petroleum Mining Act: commonly cited in secondary literature but not verified in an opened source (US Bureau of Mines Minerals Yearbook pages at search.library.wisc.edu were access-blocked).
- **Sarawak Oil Mining Ordinance 1958** exact date and number; **Sabah Teiseki Oil Company licence, May 1969** (east coast Sabah — Sulu/Celebes side, Abdullah & Lai p. 193) — mentioned but not quoted in detail.
- **Malaysian diplomatic protest text against RA 5446 (1968)** and Malaysian UN statements of 15–16 Oct 1968 (referred to by Fernandez via Tolentino's reply) — content not opened; may contain language on territorial waters around Sabah.
- **1960s Malaysian patrols, surveys or statements on James Shoal, Luconia Shoals, Swallow Reef, Commodore Reef or Amboyna Cay**: none found in any source consulted. The earliest documented activity is the 1974 RMN directive / 1975 marker expedition (Lai, Kuik & Abdullah 2021), outside the baseline window.
