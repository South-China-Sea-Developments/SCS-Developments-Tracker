# Gaps: Malaysia (MY), 1990–1999

Companion to `20261107-MY-1990s.json` (29 candidate events). These are developments the compiler knows of but could not compile: the source could not be reached, there was no usable date, the excerpt could not be checked, or a rule excluded them although a coder might still want them. One bullet each, by year.

## 1990
- **June 1990, Swallow Reef.** The CDiSS volume (Yasin et al., in Hamzah et al. 2020, ch. 1) says the resort boat *Sin Matu* took two Japanese divers out in June 1990 and a PASKAL rescue team was then sent. This is a search-and-rescue episode, not an assertion. Left out.
- **Date conflict, Layang-Layang resort.** The JSON dates the opening to 15 January 1990 (Lai et al. 2021; CDiSS). *The Christian Science Monitor* (1 December 1993) says the resort opened "this year", meaning 1993. A coder may want to check this in the NST archive.

## 1991
- **Early July 1991, Malaysia.** In the Dewan Rakyat on 19 July 1991, MP Mohamad Sabu said the *Far Eastern Economic Review* issue of 4 July 1991 had been banned in Malaysia. He linked the ban to a report that Chinese troops had landed on Swallow Reef. This is domestic censorship, not a claim act, and the ban's date is not confirmed. The Deputy Foreign Minister's reply that day was conciliatory. Source: Hansard, Dewan Rakyat, 1991-07-19 (FEER Archive, ProQuest, for the issue itself).
- **1991, Swallow Reef airstrip protests.** Vietnam, China and other claimants protested the airstrip in 1991 (Hansard, 1991-11-28 and 1991-12-05; FEER). These are other states' acts, so they are not events. The protests' dates are not known.
- **1991-01-10, Swallow Reef.** A backbencher mentioned a $27 million allocation for the commando base or development on Terumbu Layang-Layang. No government confirmation was found and the budget year is unclear. Left out. Source: Hansard, Dewan Rakyat, 1991-01-10.

## 1992
- **1992 (no date), Malaysia to China.** Malaysia "sought formal clarification when China promulgated its Law on the Territorial Sea and the Contiguous Zone in 1992" (Lai et al. 2021, p. 206). This was probably a note or démarche after 25 February 1992, but no date or text was found. It would be a separate declarative/diplomatic event, now mentioned only in the reasoning of the 1992-04-28 event. Look in FBIS East Asia, March–April 1992, and Wisma Putra's *Foreign Affairs Malaysia*.
- **1992-05-11, 1992-06-01 and 1992-06-02, Hansard.** Repeated rejections of China's claim (Dewan Rakyat 11 May; Dewan Negara 2 June). On 1 June, Najib told the Dewan Negara that the armed forces had contingency plans against intrusion at the Spratly outposts. These were folded into the 1992-04-28 event rather than listed separately.
- **1992-12-01, Hansard.** The Foreign Ministry wrote that Malaysia "had officially rejected" other states' claims. This repeats the July 1992 formula, so it was not listed.
- **5 June 1992, Malaysia–Vietnam provisional arrangement.** Cooperative, so excluded.

## 1993
- **12 May 1993.** Mahathir told Vietnam's First Deputy Prime Minister that the parties should not bring historical arguments into South China Sea disputes (Dzurek 1996, citing BERNAMA). This is conciliatory or procedural. Excluded.
- **4 June 1993, Malaysia–Vietnam joint development notes (Gulf of Thailand).** Cooperative, so excluded.
- **1993, fisheries.** The JSON's 1993 enforcement figure (606 fishermen, 85 vessels) is not broken down by nationality or area. The Department of Fisheries annual reports would be needed for Vietnamese or Chinese counts in South China Sea waters.

## 1994
- **1994, Swallow Reef.** The CDiSS volume says TNB chairman Ani Arope visited Swallow Reef to inspect works (no day). This is minor infrastructure. Left out.
- **18 May 1994, Mariveles Reef.** The protected-zone declaration for Station Mike is in the JSON, but its only source is the CDiSS PDF, which returns HTTP 404 to direct download and could be read only through WebFetch. The gazette notice under the Protected Areas and Places Act was not found. It should be confirmed from the *Warta Kerajaan*.

## 1995
- **March 1995, Chinese fishing boat fired on.** This is in the JSON at low confidence, from a single scholarly line (Novicio 2003, IDSS Monograph 5). The day, place, vessel, casualties and Chinese protest are all unknown. Search FBIS East Asia and Reuters/UPI for late March 1995. UPI Archives returned 403 and no wire copy was found.
- **31 May – 2 June 1995, Swallow Reef statements.** These are in the JSON through Dzurek's chronology only. The underlying reports are *Berita Harian* 1 June 1995, "Minister: Claim to Island Indisputable" (FBIS East Asia, 1 June 1995, pp. 59–60), and *Berita Harian* 2 June 1995, "Layang-Layang: China Is Aggravating the Situation" (FBIS, 2 June 1995, pp. 65–66). Neither the minister nor the day was confirmed. This may be linked to Mahathir's May 1995 visit.
- **May 1995, Mahathir visit and exercise.** The JSON has this at month precision (Lai et al., citing Wu 2013 and Storey 2011). The day of the visit, the name and dates of the "large-scale military exercise" and the increase in patrols were not found. Check the NST/Berita Harian archive (NSTP KLIK) and FBIS for May 1995.
- **19 June 1995, Dewan Rakyat.** A Foreign Ministry answer on "the claims and position of the nation on the Spratly Islands" was given on 19 June 1995 (referred to on 29 June 1995). It is not found in the digital Hansard text for that day and may be a written answer missing from the scan.
- **15–16 July 1995.** *Utusan Malaysia* carried "Foreign Minister Comments on Spratlys Incidents" (15 July) and "Military Intelligence Chief on Spratly Dispute" (16 July), translated in FBIS East Asia, 18 July 1995, pp. 43–44. Their content is unknown and FBIS was not reachable.
- **February–March 1995, Mischief Reef reactions.** Malaysia's own statement on China's Mischief Reef structures was not found. The ASEAN foreign ministers' statement of 18 March 1995 is collective and not Malaysian. A Reuters report of 16 June 1995, "Malaysia Calls for Formal Talks over Spratlys", is conciliatory.
- **29 June 1995, Hansard.** Deputy Foreign Minister Leo Michael Toyad said the features Malaysia claims lie outside the Spratly area. This repeats the October 1994 formula and is mentioned in that event's reasoning.

## 1996
- **14 October 1996, UNCLOS ratification.** This is in the JSON as declarative because of the accompanying declarations. A coder may class ratification itself as cooperative. The CDiSS volume gives 16 October 1996; the UN Treaty Collection gives 14 October.
- **1996, Syed Hamid interview.** As Defence Minister, Syed Hamid Albar called China a "sober and responsible regional player" in an Asian Defence Journal interview (quoted by Novicio 2003). This is conciliatory. Excluded.
- **9 April 1996, Hansard.** Abdullah Fadzil said the armed forces and Marine Police patrolled and visited Malaysia's islands from time to time. This is generic and mostly about piracy. Not listed.

## 1997
- **Nothing found.** The digital Hansard has 1997 sittings, but no government answer on the Spratlys, Swallow Reef or South China Sea claims turned up. No dated Malaysian act was found in the scholarly chronologies (Dzurek ends in 1996; Lai et al. and CDiSS have no 1997 entry). Chinese Premier Li Peng's joint-development proposal in Kuala Lumpur in August 1997 is China's act.

## 1998
- **March 1998 (or late 1998), Investigator Shoal and Erica Reef.** Jusoff et al. (*Journal of Law and Conflict Resolution*, academicjournals.org), citing Chung (2004), say the Philippine military found Malaysia building structures on the two features in March 1998. Foreign Minister Abdullah Badawi reportedly told Manila the work had not been authorised. Neither the date nor what Malaysia built in 1998 could be confirmed. If confirmed, it would be an earlier demonstrative (construction) event.
- **November 1998, Mischief Reef.** Foreign Minister Syed Hamid commended China's restraint when the Mischief Reef dispute flared again (Novicio 2003). This is conciliatory toward China. Excluded.
- **20 October 1998, Hansard.** The Deputy Defence Minister said new patrol vessels would let the Navy cover the EEZ in the South China Sea. This is procurement, not an act in the disputed area. Not listed.

## 1999
- **Date conflict for the occupations.** Lai et al. give 24 April 1999 for both reefs. The CDiSS volume gives module arrivals on 10 May (Investigator Shoal) and 12 May (Erica Reef), after towing from mid-April. NST (2019) gives "April 1999" for Station Papa and "May 10, 1999" for the establishment of Station Sierra. The JSON uses the CDiSS and NST dates and records the conflict in reasoning.
- **June 1999, Mahathir statement.** Mahathir said Malaysia was not trespassing and that the structures were for climatic research, marine-life studies and navigational aids (Jusoff et al., citing Chung 2004 and Emmers). President Estrada replied that the Philippines might build its own structures. No dated primary or press source was reachable, so this is not compiled. It would be a declarative MP event. Check the Straits Times/Business Times on NewspaperSG and *Philippine Daily Inquirer* or *Philstar* archives for June–July 1999.
- **June–July 1999, Malaysian replies to Philippine protest notes.** The Philippines sent a diplomatic protest saying Malaysia had trespassed on Investigator Shoal and breached the 1992 Manila Declaration (Jusoff et al.). Any Malaysian note in reply was not found. There is also a reported Malaysian statement that the works were within its EEZ.
- **23–26 July 1999, ASEAN ministerial meeting and ARF, Singapore.** Syed Hamid rejected Philippine requests to discuss the Spratlys at the ARF and opposed the Philippine–Vietnamese code-of-conduct draft (Novicio 2003). This is diplomatic positioning rather than an assertion of Malaysia's claim. Excluded, but a coder may weigh it. The Dewan Negara (26 and 29 July 1999) recorded the government's line that the issue should stay off the ARF agenda.
- **Late October 1999, Malaysian Defence Ministry.** After the 28 October air incident, the Defence Ministry said "it was not a stand-off" (US EIA, South China Sea Tables and Maps, 2003). The exact date and wording of the original statement were not found.
- **13 July 1999 and 28 June 1999.** Taiwan's statement denouncing Malaysian and Philippine actions, and Vietnam's protest, are other states' acts and are not events. They are mentioned only as context.
- **Budget 2000 allocation for Pulau Layang-Layang.** This is in the JSON at the date of the 9 November 1999 debate. The budget's tabling date (late October 1999) and the line item were not checked against the estimates.

## Sources that could not be reached
- **NewspaperSG** (Straits Times, Business Times, Berita Harian Singapore). An AWS WAF challenge blocked curl, and WebFetch is not allow-listed. This was the main hoped-for press archive for 1990–1999.
- **UPI Archives** (upi.com/Archives). HTTP 403 to both curl and WebFetch.
- **NSTP KLIK** (NST and Berita Harian Malaysia archive). Paywalled.
- **FBIS Daily Reports** (Readex) and the **FEER Archive** (ProQuest). Library-only. FBIS citations come second-hand through Dzurek (1996).
- **Keesing's Record of World Events** and **Southeast Asian Affairs** (ISEAS, Project MUSE). Library or paywalled.
- **CDiSS/UPNM volume** (*Malaysia and South China Sea: Policy, Strategy and Risks*). Direct download returns HTTP 404 and the Wayback Machine is blocked by egress policy. It was read only through WebFetch summaries, so its excerpts are short quotations returned by WebFetch and need checking.
- **IBRU Boundary and Security Bulletin** quarterly news sections (Durham), 1993–2000. Only feature articles were found, and the www.durham.ac.uk host returns 403 to curl. The Dzurek briefing downloaded from dur.ac.uk with a browser user agent.
- **Stratfor/RANE Worldview** 1999 analyses. Paywalled; only the summary metadata was readable.
- **Australian Parliamentary Library South China Sea chronology.** Reachable through WebFetch, but it has only one Malaysia entry for the decade (October 1996 UNCLOS ratification).
- **The Asan Forum** (403) and **GlobalSecurity.org** (402 to curl).
