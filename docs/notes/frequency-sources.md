# Frequency sources: where the numbers for a frequency move live

A *frequency* move (coding guide, Part II) needs two comparable figures for the same kind of act in
consecutive years — or a source in a position to know that states the increase. Chubb never counted
incidents off the news; he cited series that count (the SOA yearbooks' patrol figures, Vietnam's incident
tallies, the CCTV *Lan jiang weishi* retrospectives) and press that said "stepped up". This note is the
index of such series, so that a coder who sees "more of the same" in the candidates knows where to look
in minutes rather than hours. `docs/reports/candidate-tallies.md` (regenerated by `python -m pipeline.tally`)
shows where in the harvest a practice *looks* more frequent; it is a pointer to this page, never a figure
to cite.

How to use it in a sitting: decide the candidate as a continuation and put the figure you saw in the note
(`continuation of CN200802 — Vietnam reports 23 incidents Jan–Jun 2016`). When the year's rows are done
and the notes add up to a documented increase, return to the best-sourced candidate, make it the frequency
move with the two figures in its description, and merge the others into it. Add the series you used here.

Entries marked **(verify)** are from the maintainers' knowledge of the field, not yet checked in this
project: confirm the series exists for the years you need and what exactly it counts before citing it.
Two series that count different things (incidents reported to a ministry vs. ships seen by satellite) do
not make a pair.

## Across claimants

- **AMTI (CSIS Asia Maritime Transparency Initiative)** — satellite-based counts since 2016: vessels present
  at named features by day (Scarborough, Second Thomas, Thitu, Whitsun/Julian Felipe, Luconia), CCG and
  militia deployment tallies, island-building footprints, and periodic "year in review" pieces. The best
  single source for *presence* and *deployment* frequency 2016–. **(verify** the feature and the years: coverage
  is feature-by-feature, not continuous for every one.)
- **SCSPI (South China Sea Strategic Situation Probing Initiative, Peking University)** — AIS-based monthly
  counts of US military aircraft and ship activity in the SCS, and of vessel presence, from 2019. A Chinese
  institute: usable for US-directed frequency when paired with its own earlier figures. **(verify)**
- **US Department of Defense annual report to Congress on China (Military and Security Developments
  Involving the PRC)** — yearly narrative with occasional counts of exercises, coast guard growth, "unsafe
  intercepts" (the 2022–23 reports gave totals of intercepts of US aircraft over two years). **(verify** which
  year's report gives numbers.)
- **Japan MOD "China's Activities in the South China Sea"** and Japan's defence white paper — annual
  summaries with counts of Chinese construction and deployments. **(verify)**
- **IISS Military Balance / Strategic Survey**; **ONI "PLA Navy" reports** — force structure counts (hulls,
  coast guard tonnage), useful for the scale of a practice rather than its incidents. **(verify)**
- **Chubb's own sources for 1970–2015** (Appendix II of *Chinese Expansion and the SCS* and the IS 2021
  codebook): *Zhongguo haiyang nianjian* (ZGHYNJ, SOA yearbook) for rights-defence patrol counts;
  *Zhongguo yuye nianjian* (ZGYYNJ, fisheries yearbook) for expulsions and seizures; CCTV *Lan jiang weishi*;
  NISCSS annual assessments; *People's Daily* article counts (CN199901 "ninefold"). Several run past 2015
  and remain the natural comparator for a China frequency claim. See the generated section below.

## China

- **China Coast Guard annual summaries** (CCG website / Xinhua year-end pieces) — counts of "rights
  protection" law-enforcement patrols, vessels expelled, boats boarded. From 2013 (CCG formed) and more
  regular from 2018 (CCG under the People's Armed Police). **(verify** year by year: not every year has a
  published total.)
- **Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs / fisheries yearbook (ZGYYNJ)** — fishing-ban enforcement
  figures ("summer fishing moratorium": ships deployed, boats inspected, violations) annually. The series
  Chubb used for CN200802's 100+ → 313. **(verify** continuation past 2015.)
- **Ministry of National Defense monthly press conferences** — announce major exercises; counting them is
  Chubb's CN201302 method ("at least 12" in 2013 vs 5–6).
- **MFA regular press conferences** (fmprc.gov.cn archive) — for statement frequency: how many times a
  year the spokesperson addresses a named feature or claimant. Countable from the archive; no series counts
  it for you.
- **AMTI** for island-building footprints (CN201403/CN201504 were coded from it) and feature presence.

## Vietnam

- **Vietnam Fisheries Surveillance / Directorate of Fisheries (Tổng cục Thủy sản) and the Vietnam Fisheries
  Society (VINAFIS)** — periodic counts of fishing boats attacked, chased or robbed by "foreign vessels",
  usually reported in *Tuổi Trẻ*, *Thanh Niên*, *VnExpress*; Quảng Ngãi province (Lý Sơn) reports its own
  figures for the Paracels. The series Chubb used for 2015's "three coercive actions" and the 2007–2008
  comparison. **(verify** the reporting unit — incidents vs boats vs fishermen — before pairing years.)
- **Vietnam Coast Guard / Ministry of Defence white papers** (2009, 2019) — fleet and patrol scale. **(verify)**
- **MFA spokesperson statements** (mofa.gov.vn archive) — countable per year by feature.

## Philippines

- **National Task Force for the West Philippine Sea (NTF-WPS), Philippine Coast Guard, BFAR** — since 2021,
  near-weekly counts of Chinese vessels at Julian Felipe (Whitsun), Sabina, Scarborough, Second Thomas;
  PCG monthly "maritime domain awareness" releases 2023–. The richest Philippine presence series. **(verify**
  the start date per feature.)
- **DFA diplomatic protests** — the DFA publishes running totals ("the Philippines has filed N diplomatic
  protests this year/since 2016", with yearly figures from 2020). A ready-made statement-frequency series for
  PH, and a proxy for China's acts as seen by Manila. **(verify** the annual breakdown.)
- **AFP Western Command** — counts of Chinese vessels sighted in the Kalayaan group, reported to Congress and
  the press, 2019–. **(verify)**
- **AMTI** for Scarborough and Second Thomas presence 2016–.

## Malaysia

- **Malaysian Maritime Enforcement Agency (MMEA/APMM) and the Royal Malaysian Navy** — annual arrests of
  foreign fishing boats (the 2006 "315 foreign fishermen arrested" figure in the harvest is of this kind);
  occasional counts of Chinese coast guard intrusions at Luconia (the 2020 "89 intrusions in four years"
  Auditor-General figure). **(verify** the Auditor-General's report year and scope.)
- **Parliamentary written answers (Dewan Rakyat Hansard)** — ministers give counts when asked; searchable.
- **AMTI** for Luconia Shoals presence.

## Indonesia

- **Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (KKP / PSDKP)** — annual counts of foreign fishing boats seized
  and sunk, by flag, 2014– (the "sink the boats" policy produced a well-published series). Also Bakamla
  annual reports 2015–. **(verify** by-area breakdown: Natuna vs the rest of the EEZ.)
- **Indonesian Navy (TNI AL) Fleet Command I** — statements on Natuna patrols and Chinese CCG presence 2016–.
- **AMTI** and SCSPI for CCG/survey-ship presence off Natuna 2019–.

## Taiwan

- **Coast Guard Administration (CGA) annual statistics and the Ocean Affairs Council** — counts of mainland
  fishing boats and dredgers expelled or detained around Pratas (Dongsha) and in Taiwan's waters; Pratas and
  Itu Aba (Taiping) patrol and supply mission counts in Legislative Yuan reports. **(verify** that Pratas is
  broken out separately.)
- **MND daily PLA activity reports** (2020–) — aircraft and ships around Taiwan, including the Pratas sector;
  countable by day. **(verify** whether the SCS sector is separable.)
- **Legislative Yuan interpellations** — ministers' answers carry counts.

## United States (as counterpart)

- **US Navy / INDOPACOM FONOP annual reports to Congress** — the DoD Freedom of Navigation Report lists each
  year's challenged claims by country; the Navy's SCS FONOPs are also tallied in the press (e.g., by year
  2015–). The denominator for "more intercepts of US ships" claims. **(verify** the fiscal-year basis.)
- **SCSPI** monthly counts of US reconnaissance flights (from 2019), for the same reason.
